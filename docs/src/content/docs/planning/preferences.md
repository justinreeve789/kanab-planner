---
title: Your preferences explained
description: What adventurousness, solitariness, strenuousness and hiking pace actually change.
---

Every trail, restaurant, event and place in Trip Planner has its own adventurousness, solitariness and strenuousness score between 0 and 1. Your three sliders say where you'd like to be on each scale, and Trip Planner picks the things whose scores sit closest to yours.

The sliders also shape the trip as a whole. Here's what each one does.

## Adventurousness

How far you'll go, and how full you like your days.

- **Trail days.** At the low end, about a quarter of your full days include a hike. In the middle, about half. At the top, every full day.
- **More than one hike a day.** At about 70% and above, a trail day can include a second hike, or a short evening walk.
- **Distance from base.** Below 30%, day trips stay within about 60 miles of your base; below 40%, within about 75 miles. Higher settings open up Page, Lake Powell, St. George, Bryce Canyon and the North Rim.
- **Repeat visits.** Below 80%, farther-flung areas are visited at most once. Kanab and Zion can come up on any day.
- **Events.** Local events are only added from 50% up.
- **Meals out.** At 60% and above you'll sometimes have breakfast at a café instead of at home. Above 30%, dinner is usually at a restaurant.
- **Trail length.** Low adventurousness (or low strenuousness) caps how long a trail can be. High adventurousness (or high strenuousness) sets a minimum, so you don't get a string of short strolls.

## Solitariness

How much you'd like to avoid crowds. Higher settings favour trails and places that tend to be quieter. Lower settings favour popular, lively spots. Crowd levels come from how busy each place typically is.

## Strenuousness

How hard you want to work. A trail's strenuousness score combines its length, total climb, how steep it is on average, how long it takes and its official difficulty rating. Strenuousness carries the most weight of the three when choosing trails.

- Low settings cap trail length and favour flat, easy walks.
- High settings set a minimum length and favour long climbs.

## Hiking pace

The minutes-per-mile you give on the Activities step. Trip Planner walks through each trail's elevation profile in short pieces, slows down for steep climbs and descents the way real hikers do, and adds a little for tiredness on long hikes. The result is the hike time shown on your itinerary.

If hikes look too long or too short for you, change your pace in the questionnaire and build a new itinerary.

## Changing your preferences later

- Select the three coloured bars in the header to go straight to the sliders.
- Or reopen the questionnaire from the **questionnaire** button (bottom right) or **Preferences** on a phone.

New preferences apply to the next itinerary you build. Your current itinerary stays as it is until you choose **New itinerary** or the circular arrows in the header.
